As you know, we’re on the brink of an artificial intelligence revolution! Artificial intelligence is growing by leaps and bounds, and it’s beginning to integrate into our everyday lives and cybersecurity in various ways. AI will likely change our lives and our work in ways that are hard to anticipate.

Do not fret, I _can_ tell you one thing with certainty: AI will not make cybersecurity engineers obsolete. Artificial intelligence will make you better at your job if you embrace it and learn to use it properly and efficiently.

Here are a few reasons AI will not replace all cybersecurity professionals:

- **AI Augments Rather Than Replaces Cybersecurity Roles**
- AI can automate certain routine tasks and enhance threat detection capabilities, but it can’t fully replace the _critical thinking_, _creativity_, and _decision-making skills_ of human cybersecurity professionals. AI lacks the intuition, context understanding, and ability to devise the innovative solutions that are essential in cybersecurity.
- **AI Has Limitations**
- AI algorithms are trained on existing data and may struggle to detect new or sophisticated cyber threats that haven’t been seen before. Cyber-criminals can create attacks designed to bypass AI systems, necessitating human intervention and ingenuity to respond effectively. In this circumstance, AI will necessitate _additional_, well-trained cybersecurity professionals.
- **Cybersecurity Requires Human Expertise**
- Cybersecurity is an ever-evolving field that demands _continuous learning_ (see the Hackers-Arise Lifetime training program), adaptation, and ethical decision-making. While AI can assist in automating tasks, it can’t replace the human expertise required to understand the context, evaluate the effectiveness of security measures, and make decisions based on legal and ethical considerations (see the Hackers-Arise Criminal Law for Cybersecurity Professionals training program).
- **New Roles Will Emerge**
- As AI becomes more prevalent in cybersecurity, new roles and specializations will emerge, such as AI security analysts, machine learning security engineers, and professionals responsible for developing, training, implementing, and securing AI systems. These roles will complement and work alongside traditional cybersecurity roles. These positions will likely have even higher salaries than the traditional cybersecurity engineers.

## Collaboration Is Key

The most effective approach to leveraging AI revolution in cybersecurity will be the collaboration between AI and human cybersecurity professionals. AI can _augment_ human capabilities by automating routine tasks, enabling professionals to focus on more complex and strategic aspects of cybersecurity. Humans will oversee and guide AI systems to ensure ethical and effective cybersecurity practices.

While AI will transform and enhance various aspects of cybersecurity, it’s highly unlikely to make human cybersecurity engineers obsolete in the next 5 to 10 years. The dynamic nature of cyber threats, the limitations of AI, and the need for human expertise and ethical decision-making will ensure that cybersecurity remains a field that requires skilled professionals working in tandem with AI technologies.

## Social Engineering Attacks with AI

Over 80 percent of all successful cyberattacks include an element of social engineering. Social engineering relies upon manipulating human vulnerabilities rather than technical vulnerabilities. Tactics like phishing, email impersonation, and pretexting are becoming increasingly sophisticated and prevalent. For instance, ransomware attacks, presently the most successful financial attacks, usually begin with someone within the targeted organization opening a file or clicking a link. This simple action gives that attacker an opening into the organization, allowing them to encrypt all the data in the organization, which leads to the crippling of the organization. Eventually, the company must pay the ransom to get its data back.

In the initial phases of AI in cybersecurity, attackers will likely focus on using AI to craft email messages that entice their targets to click a link or open a document. Writing email may be one of the best uses for artificial intelligence for hackers right now, as AI can gather the key information and draft an excellent and effective email to the intended targets.

## Using AI to Write a Bash Script

In [Chapter 8](https://learning.oreilly.com/library/view/linux-basics-for/9798341655102/xhtml/ch08.xhtml#ch08), we looked at some basics of bash scripting. Bash scripting can make your life so much more efficient, as you can write a small script to do repetitive tasks that a machine does much better. You also can use AI to write these scripts, even if your scripting skills are limited.

For instance, let’s ask our favorite AI application to write a script to use a cron job to back up our data every Saturday night at 11:30 PM (presumably when no one is on the network). Backing up your data is your best prevention from the damage done by ransomware. Here’s the script:

```
#!/bin/bash

# Set the source directory to backup
SOURCE_DIR="/path/to/your/data"

# Set the backup destination
BACKUP_DIR="/path/to/backup/location"

# Create a timestamp for the backup file
TIMESTAMP=$(date +"%Y%m%d_%H%M%S")

# Set the backup filename
BACKUP_FILE="backup_${TIMESTAMP}.tar.gz"

# Create the backup directory if it doesn't exist
mkdir -p "$BACKUP_DIR"

# Create the backup
tar -czf "${BACKUP_DIR}/${BACKUP_FILE}" "$SOURCE_DIR"

# Optional: Remove backups older than 30 days
find "$BACKUP_DIR" -name "backup_*.tar.gz" -mtime +30 -delete

# Optional: Log the backup operation
echo "Backup completed on $(date)" >> "${BACKUP_DIR}/backup_log.txt"
```
The AI then goes on to remind us to change the permissions:

```
chmod +x /path/to/backup_script.sh
then edit our cron table
crontab -e
30 23 * * 6 /path/to/backup_script.sh
```

Now, we should be able to back up our system automatically. Isn’t AI wonderful?
